Section~S1 describes the record, its provenance and the population, and repeats the measurements on all handles. Sections~S2 to S4 follow the three choices of an agent, where to write, how to sign and how to write, with the choice of the windows, the classification of name pieces, the selection of conventions and the individual response curves. \section{Data provenance, task families and population}\label{sec:population}

\paragraph{Provenance.} Everything we know about the agents comes from the record and from the report that released it (ref.~1 of the paper, Von Arx et al., \texttt{collusion.wiki}, 4 September 2026); no transcript, prompt or model identity is available. The release has 14,591 stored revisions of 4,579 pages, with the timestamp, the username, the text added and the page after the edit. The histories omit edits below 64 characters on DSEWiki and the deleted pages that could not be recovered, and a username is not necessarily a single run. The report establishes that the edits were made through plain read requests, which these wikis accept as edits, and that 98.5\% of the edits on DSEWiki came from Microsoft Azure, the cloud infrastructure that OpenAI uses; that the task was a series of timed questions, usually five, on public statistics sites such as DataUSA, OECD and IHME, with a long first window and short later ones; and that the agents signed with self-chosen names. It infers from the content of the pages that each handle was a sandboxed run that ended with its task. It does not state which model produced the edits, whether the task was part of training or of evaluation, or what the agents read beyond what they wrote: the server log of the release records saves, deletions and probes, not page reads. We assume what the report argues and no more. Three accounts flagged as human and revisions without a username are excluded, which leaves 13,661 edits by 3,099 handles.

\paragraph{Task families and population.} A page belongs to a task family when its questions or its name identify one data-lookup task, such as the DataUSA clothing workforce task or the OECD Equity task; the 41 families are listed in Table~\ref{tab:families}. The population of the paper is the 1,201 handles that wrote at least once on a task page: 5,929 edits, of which 3,807 on 679 task pages. A handle is assigned to the family of its first task-page edit; 945 handles wrote on one family, 168 on two and 88 on three or more. The largest family gathered 213 handles, and 795 of the 1,201 handles worked on at least one of the seven largest.

\paragraph{Spans and activity.} A handle's activity span, from its first to its last edit, has a median of 2.02~h among the 827 handles with at least two edits (the 374 handles with one edit have no span); this is the window within which a model agent makes its two edits (Section~S6). 29\% of these spans exceed 6~h and 8.7\% a day, a tail that comes from runs that renamed themselves and from a few generic names that were reused. A handle made a median of 3 edits, 31\% made one, and the most active made 176. Pages were written on for a median of 1.1~h, but stayed readable for a median of 17 days: of the 1,710 pages touched by the population, 1,692 were eventually deleted by the moderator, and 95\% were still readable one day after their creation.

\section{Reconstructed exposure and page choices}\label{sec:pages}

\paragraph{The feed.} The record stores every revision with its time, so the RecentChanges listing can be rebuilt at any instant: it is the list of the latest revisions across the wiki, by any handle, including handles outside the population. Which part of it an agent requested or read is not logged. The paper takes the last 100 revisions as the potentially visible feed, and the page being edited, as it was before the edit, as the potentially visible page. An \emph{arrival} is the first edit of a handle on a task page; of the 2,612 arrivals, 679 create a page and 1,933 append to an existing one.

\paragraph{The window.} Figure~\ref{fig:pages_windows}a repeats the response curve of Fig.~\ref{fig2}a with windows of 15, 30, 100 and 300 lines. The share of appends that land on a page inside the window rises from 57\% at 15 lines to 72\% at 30, 89\% at 100 and 95\% at 300, and the fitted slope is 0.76, 0.86, 0.87 and 0.58. A short window truncates the choice set, so that many of the pages actually chosen are not candidates, and a long window dilutes recency, so that a page holding a small share of 300 lines is chosen more often than its share and a page holding a large share less often. The response is closest to proportional between 30 and 100 lines, and at 100 lines most of the choices are inside the window.

\paragraph{The model window.} Figure~\ref{fig:pages_windows}b repeats the model of Fig.~\ref{fig3}a with the agents reading the last 30, 100, 300 or 1,000 feed lines. The probability that a page gathers at least 5, 10, 20 or 40 handles is 0.23, 0.093, 0.027 and 0.002 with 30 lines, 0.21, 0.087, 0.029 and 0.004 with 100 (medians over 10 runs; the 200 runs of Fig.~\ref{fig3}a give 0.030 for 20 handles), 0.19, 0.083, 0.025 and 0.007 with 300, and 0.16, 0.056, 0.022 and 0.009 with 1,000, against 0.21, 0.097, 0.031 and 0.007 observed. The body of the distribution hardly depends on the window; the largest page does, with medians of 48, 80, 126 and 210 handles against 54 observed. Over 200 runs with 100 lines the largest page has a median of 64 and a 5th to 95th percentile of 46 to 99.

\paragraph{What the proportional choice adds.} The model takes from the record only the number of agents and the number of pages an agent writes on, and its heavy tail comes from the choice of a line of the feed. Replacing it by a uniform choice among the distinct visible pages, the probability that a page gathers at least 10, 20 or 40 handles is 0.060, 0.003 and 0.000 and the largest page has 24 handles; with a uniform choice among all existing pages the figures are 0.068, 0.003 and 0.000, and 24 handles. The tail requires that a page be chosen in proportion to the lines it holds, which is what Fig.~\ref{fig2}a measures.

\paragraph{The creation rate.} The model creates a page with a constant probability $p_c=679/2612=0.260$. Over the 2,612 arrivals this probability does not depend on how many pages of the same task were already active: it is 0.18 when one or two pages of the task had been edited in the previous 24~h, 0.23 for three to five, 0.22 for six to eleven, 0.28 for twelve to twenty-four and 0.27 beyond, and between 0.23 and 0.33 on every day. The exception is the case in which no page of the task is active, 63 decisions in which an agent created a page 83\% of the time, because there was nothing to append to. A handle's first task page is created more often than its later ones, 0.34 against 0.20, and the model uses the pooled rate. Of the 2,612 arrivals, 2,474 were taken with a page of the handle's own task visible in the last 100 lines, and 24\% of them created a page, while the 138 taken with none visible created one 59\% of the time. With two pages per agent the model makes about 630 pages (626 on average over 200 runs), against 679 in the record.

\paragraph{The agent's own task.} Of the 1,411 arrivals after a handle's first, 63\% fell on a page of the handle's own family. Among visible candidates, an own-task page holding 1\% of the last 100 lines is chosen 3.9\% of the time against 0.5\% for a page of another task; at 2.3\% of the lines the two figures are 8.3\% and 1.0\%, at 5.7\% they are 20\% and 2.5\%, and at 13\% they are 35\% and 4.5\%. The response is proportional in both cases, with a slope about ten times larger for the agent's own task (Table~\ref{tab:reg_pages}). The model has a single task and ignores this preference.

\paragraph{Attachment.} Figure~\ref{fig:pages_attachment}a measures the preferential-attachment kernel: for every append to a page new to the handle, every task page edited in the previous 24~h is a candidate, and $A(k)$ is the probability that a candidate with $k$ handles already is the one chosen, over 317,679 candidate page-moments. Relative to a page with one handle, the rate is 2.0, 3.1, 4.9 and 6.7 at $k$ of 2, 3, about 4 and about 7, and then flat at about 6 out to $k$ of 25. A page with thirty handles is not chosen more often than a page with seven. Figure~\ref{fig:pages_attachment}b gives the full cross-table among candidate pages inside the last 100 feed lines. At a fixed number of handles, the probability of being chosen rises about tenfold from one line to fifteen; at a fixed number of lines it barely moves, 0.06, 0.06, 0.06, 0.05 and 0.07 at three or four lines for pages with 2--3, 4--6, 7--11, 12--24 and 25 or more handles. The saturation of panel a is what this produces once pages that are no longer in the feed enter the denominator: a page with many handles is chosen only while it is visible. The benchmark here is linear preferential attachment on cumulative audience; the regression of Section~S5 adds the number of handles to the feed share and finds that, once visibility is accounted for, audience adds almost nothing.

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering\includegraphics[width=0.75\textwidth]{figS_pages_attachment.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Attachment is linear in visibility, not in audience.} (a) The rate at which a page gains a new handle, relative to a page with one handle, against the number of handles already on it; candidates are the task pages edited in the previous 24~h; 95\% Wilson intervals; the dotted line is linear preferential attachment. (b) Probability that a candidate inside the last 100 feed lines is the page chosen, by the number of lines it holds (rows) and the number of handles already on it (columns); cells with fewer than 100 candidates are blank.}\label{fig:pages_attachment}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The branching approximation.} The Borel distribution of Fig.~\ref{fig3}a treats each feed line as picked by later edits a Poisson number of times with mean $1-p_c$. In the model a line is visible for 100 edits and each of them picks it with probability $(1-p_c)/100$ at most, the visits of an agent to distinct pages and the finite window make the picks dependent, and the distribution counts handles per page only because a model agent writes on a page once. It is an approximation to the model, not an exact distribution of the record.

\section{Usernames and name features}\label{sec:names}

\paragraph{What a name is made of.} Usernames are split into capitalized words and runs of uppercase letters, and Table~\ref{tab:pieces} lists the 44 pieces carried by at least 15 handles. Thirteen are months, because June is written both as \texttt{Jun} and as \texttt{June}; they are pooled into the date stamp. The other 31 are classified by their over-representation in a single task: the share of a piece's carriers that belong to its most common task family, divided by that family's share of the population. The record leaves a gap in this number, with no piece between 3.2 and 5.4 (Table~\ref{tab:pieces}), and we cut inside it, at 4. The 16 pieces above the cut are specific to one task: words that name the task, such as \texttt{OECD} or \texttt{Grocery}, and roles coined on the pages of one task, such as \texttt{Watcher}. The 15 pieces below it are generic, such as \texttt{Open}, \texttt{AI}, \texttt{Agent}, \texttt{Research} and \texttt{Helper}. The generic pieces and the date stamp are the 16 name features of Fig.~\ref{fig2}b, Table~\ref{tab:reg} and Fig.~\ref{fig3}b. Two generic pieces sit close to the cut: \texttt{OAI} (2.7) is mostly the suffix of the name patterns of two tasks, and \texttt{Scout} (2.8) was coined in the OECD task and then adopted outside it, by 35 to 55\% of the newborns of other tasks in the blocks after 20 June. The classification does not drive the regressions: with all 31 pieces and the date stamp the coefficients of Table~\ref{tab:reg} are 0.11, 0.49, 0.20 and 0.04 (19,456 records), and with the 16 task-specific pieces alone 0.11, 0.52, 0.27 and $-0.03$. Task-specific pieces respond to the task-mates out of view as a shared prompt would make them, but still respond most to the task-mates in the feed.

\paragraph{The curve of Fig.~\ref{fig2}b.} Each record is a newcomer and one of the 16 features, with $y=1$ if the newcomer's name carries the feature. A newcomer enters with a complete 100-line feed and at least two earlier handles of its own task family, born in the previous six hours, among the handles of the feed; $x$ is the share of these task-mates that carry the feature (15,776 records). Because the 16 features have very different base rates, the curve is a component-plus-residual plot: $y$ is regressed on $x$ with one intercept per feature, and each record is plotted at $\bar y+\hat\beta(x-\bar x)+\hat\epsilon$, averaged in bins of $x$ (0--0.1, 0.1--0.25, 0.25--0.45, 0.45--0.65, 0.65--0.85, 0.85--1, bins with at least 25 records), with a normal 95\% interval of the bin mean. It is therefore not the raw proportion of names carrying the feature. The raw proportions in the same bins are 0.04, 0.13, 0.30, 0.46, 0.69 and 0.79, against 0.07, 0.13, 0.26, 0.37, 0.53 and 0.57 adjusted: the raw curve is steeper, because features that are common in a task are both frequent among the task-mates and frequent in the newcomer's name for reasons that the feature intercepts absorb.

\paragraph{The visibility test.} The regression of Table~\ref{tab:reg} splits the earlier handles into four groups (Section~S5). With predecessors of the previous six hours, the earlier task-mates on the page of the first edit carry $0.11 \pm 0.04$, the task-mates in the feed $0.44 \pm 0.04$ and the task-mates out of view $0.14 \pm 0.04$. The task-mates in the feed carry two to three times what the task-mates out of view carry at every window of 1.5 to 12~h, and the contrast between them is between 0.20 and 0.32 (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{ed:task}c; coefficients in Table~\ref{tab:reg_names}). The coefficient of the task-mates out of view grows from 0.14 to 0.21 at twelve hours, as the window takes in older task-mates whose names had more time to reach the newcomer through intermediaries. The page of the first edit carries no more than the task-mates out of view at any window: for names the channel is the feed. A newcomer saw on average 11 task-mates and 34 handles of other tasks in the feed, and 12\% of the newcomers saw no task-mate.

\paragraph{What the test can and cannot resolve.} The response of Fig.~\ref{fig2}b is pooled over 16 features and 41 tasks, and the pooled response is what is identified. With task by three-hour intercepts added to a single feature, the estimates are no longer informative, $0.04 \pm 0.13$ for \texttt{Open} and $0.01 \pm 0.14$ for the date, because within one task and one block the share among visible task-mates hardly varies; for the same reason the fully interacted specification is not identified for names. A coarser test needs no regression. Reducing each name to its skeleton, the order of its affiliation stamp, generic role piece, task-specific piece, month and number, a newcomer has the same skeleton as a task-mate in view 11.6\% of the time, as a task-mate out of view 9.3\% of the time, and as a visible handle of another task 4.0\% of the time; the difference between task-mates in and out of view is 0.023, with a 95\% interval of 0.011 to 0.034 over 555 newcomers. Most of the similarity between the names of a task comes from the task, and a small but measurable part from what the newcomer could see.

\paragraph{Who arrives first.} For the tasks with at least 40 handles and the pieces that are not task-specific and are carried by at most 15\% of the handles outside the task, 273 pairs of a task and a piece, a piece carried by one of the first five arrivals of the task is later carried by 5.4\% of the task's handles, against 2.2\% for a piece that none of the first five carried. Part of this is not influence: a piece common in a task for any reason is also more likely to appear among its first five. The placebo that takes the last five arrivals and looks backwards gives a ratio of 1.4, against 2.5 for the first five, so that the excess attributable to arriving first is less than twofold.

\section{Writing conventions and local choices}\label{sec:forms}

\paragraph{The candidates.} The candidate list was produced by reading the added text of the corpus with Claude Code, asked to list every pair of competing forms for the same thing that occurs in it, and completed by hand. It is a search for variation, not a selection on outcome: the rule below is the only step that uses measured quantities. For each pair the two forms are matched by regular expressions in the text added by each edit, excluding links; an edit is a \emph{use} when it contains strictly more instances of one form than of the other, and ties are discarded. A handle's \emph{first use} of a convention is the first of its uses. Table~\ref{tab:conventions} lists the 52 pairs considered and why 36 were dropped. A convention is kept when the record allows its two prior probabilities to be measured: at least 100 uses, a minority form in at least 5\% of the uses, and at least ten pages with five or more uses. Thirteen candidates had fewer than 100 uses. For six the minority form never occurs, as for the 24-hour clock against am and pm. For sixteen of the other seventeen one form is rare or the uses sit on a few pages, and the prior strength cannot be told from the record: when every page shows the same form, an agent that copies the page and an agent that ignores it write the same thing. The last, \texttt{\&} against \texttt{and}, is a quotation rather than a choice: 177 of the 181 instances of \texttt{\&} sit inside a category name copied from a statistics site, such as ``Visual \& Performing Arts''. The profile-likelihood interval of $s=\mu_A+\mu_B$ in Table~\ref{tab:conventions} makes the selection explicit: its median width is 0.20 for the kept conventions and 0.69 for the dropped ones with both forms used. The kept conventions have between 274 and 2,878 uses, on between 11 and 166 pages, and prior strengths between 0.02 and 0.71.

\paragraph{Each convention separately.} Figure~\ref{fig:forms_each} shows, for every kept convention, the line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:copy} at its fitted prior probabilities against the records it is fitted on: every use with at least three instances in view, where what is in view is the earlier uses on the page, the writer's own included, or the uses in the feed when the page carries neither form (Section~S6). These records populate three to five of the five bins of every convention, and the line passes inside the 95\% interval of 67 of the 74 bins. The misses are \texttt{Rn}/\texttt{\#n} and \texttt{task clock}/\texttt{scaffold}, where uses on mixed pages take the first form more often than the line, and \texttt{update}/\texttt{edit} and \texttt{LIVE}/\texttt{live}, each at one or two intermediate bins. The intervals treat uses as independent, while a handle's uses repeat its own form (Table~\ref{tab:habit}), so they are too narrow. Each handle's first use alone, the records of Fig.~\ref{fig2}c, are drawn faint: most first uses land on pages that already carry one form only, so that for seven conventions they fill a single bin, which cannot test a line. Where they fill several bins, they lie near the line for most conventions, and above it on mixed pages for the thousands separator. Over time, the coined names and near-synonyms go to one form and stay there, while the typographic habits stay split in every block of 50 newborns from the first to the last.

\paragraph{Controls on the page.} Two controls separate the page from time and from the task. First, the agreement between two uses on the same page could arise if agents born at the same time write the same way and land on the same pages; Extended Data Fig.~\ref{ed:patchwork}b compares it with 1,000 permutations of the forms within three-hour blocks, and within task family and three-hour block. Second, the page is a memory, and it should predict a use even when read well before the use. Table~\ref{tab:slopes} gives the response slopes with the page tally taken six hours before each first use: 0.90, 0.87 and 0.70 for the three classes, against 0.94, 0.79 and 0.85 at the time of the use. What an agent finds on a page is largely what was there six hours earlier, which is how a population with no memory of its own keeps a convention. With all uses in place of first uses the slopes are 0.95, 0.90 and 0.85.

\paragraph{The page against the rest of the task.} If each task family had a vocabulary of its own, the page would predict a form only because it belongs to the family. For every first use we reconstruct the share of the form on the page, the share among the last 30 uses of the convention by other handles on the \emph{other} pages of the same family, and the share among the last 30 uses anywhere (each with at least three instances). In the 204 first uses where the page majority and the family majority lie on opposite sides of one half, the handle follows the page 68\% of the time (95\% interval 61 to 74\%): 77\% for the coined names, 63\% for the near-synonyms and 67\% for the habits (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{ed:task}a). Fitting the three shares jointly on the 1,798 first uses that have all three, with standard errors clustered by handle, the page carries $0.73 \pm 0.05$, the family's other pages $0.09 \pm 0.05$ and the feed $0.11 \pm 0.05$, and the contrast page minus family is 0.63 (95\% interval 0.45 to 0.82; Extended Data Fig.~\ref{ed:task}b). The ordering holds in every class (page against family: coined names 0.86 against 0.05, near-synonyms 0.64 against 0.21, habits 0.79 against 0.08). With intercepts for every combination of convention, family and day, 434 strata, the slope of the response to the page share is $0.51 \pm 0.04$ against 0.87 without them: half of the raw association is between families and days, half between pages of the same family on the same day. For the 16 name features the first page matters less and the feed more: in a joint fit on 6,352 records the earlier same-task authors of the page carry $0.34 \pm 0.05$, the same-task handles of the previous six hours on other pages $0.22 \pm 0.06$ and the feed $0.49 \pm 0.07$; the contrast between the first two, 0.12 (95\% interval $-0.04$ to 0.28), is not significant, and when they disagree the newcomer follows the page 59\% of the time (n = 347). Conflicts with a majority at exactly one half are excluded, and the 50\% reference is descriptive: the conflicts are a selected subset of uses.

\section{Identification regressions: samples, specifications, inference}\label{sec:regressions}

\paragraph{Samples.} The response curves of Fig.~\ref{fig2} and the regressions of Table~\ref{tab:reg} use different sets of records, because a curve needs only the share it plots and a regression every share it includes. Table~\ref{tab:samples} gives the unit, the eligibility rule and the size of each.

\paragraph{Names and conventions.} For names a record is a newcomer $i$ and a feature, with $y_i=1$ if its name carries the feature; for conventions a record is a handle's first use of a convention, with $y_i=1$ if it takes form A. The outcome is the presence of the feature, or form A, not the agreement with the exposure. The regression is Eq.~\eqref{eq:reg} of the Methods,
\[
y_i=\alpha_{k(i)}+\gamma_{\tau(i),b(i)}+\beta_{\mathrm{page}}\rho_i^{\mathrm{page}}+\beta_{\mathrm{feed}}\rho_i^{\mathrm{feed}}+\beta_{\mathrm{out}}\rho_i^{\mathrm{out}}+\beta_{\mathrm{other}}\rho_i^{\mathrm{other}}+\theta m_i+\epsilon_i,
\]
with $\alpha_k$ an intercept per feature or convention and $\gamma_{\tau,b}$ an intercept per task family and three-hour block of the newcomer's birth or of the use. For names the four shares are fractions of handles carrying the feature among: the earlier same-task authors of the newcomer's first page ($\rho^{\mathrm{page}}$); the same-task handles in the last 100 feed lines ($\rho^{\mathrm{feed}}$); the same-task handles first observed in the previous six hours that are neither in the feed nor among the page's authors ($\rho^{\mathrm{out}}$); and the handles of other tasks in the feed ($\rho^{\mathrm{other}}$). For conventions they are fractions of uses taking form A among the earlier uses on the page, the uses of the convention by same-task handles in the last 100 feed lines, the uses by same-task handles in the previous six hours outside the page and the feed, and the uses by other tasks in the feed; a group can contain several uses by one handle. Each share requires at least two handles or uses. For names a newcomer whose first page has fewer than two earlier same-task authors, which is the case when it created the page, keeps its record with $\rho^{\mathrm{page}}=0$ and $m_i=1$; $\theta$ is estimated and reported (Table~\ref{tab:reg_names}). For conventions no such indicator enters: the record requires all four groups. A handle can appear both among the authors of a page and in the feed, so the page and feed shares are not disjoint for names; the out-of-view group is disjoint from both by construction. These shares are potential exposure: the record shows what an agent could have read, not what it read.

\paragraph{Pages.} A record is a candidate page $j$ of an append $d$, with $y_{dj}=1$ for the page chosen, and the regression is Eq.~\eqref{eq:page_reg} with an intercept per append. The candidates are the task pages in the last 100 feed lines and the earlier pages of the handle's own task that are no longer in the feed; each candidate appears once per append, and every regressor is computed from the revisions before the append. In 39 of the 1,933 appends the chosen page belongs to another task and is no longer in the feed, so that it is not among the candidates. These appends have no chosen alternative; they are left out rather than given one, which leaves 87,349 candidates of 1,894 appends, each with exactly one chosen candidate. Leaving them out changes the coefficients of the linear model in the third decimal, and the conditional logit coefficients of the own-task and other-task feed shares from 9.93 and 8.29 to 9.17 and 9.13. The coefficients of a linear probability model are conditional associations, not probabilities that an agent copied.

\paragraph{Remarks on the specifications.} For pages the intercept per append nests the task by three-hour effects, so there is one specification. An own-task page that is visible is chosen 8.7\% of the time (n = 15,571), and one that has scrolled out of the last 100 lines 0.5\% of the time (n = 39,431). The handles already on a page, in logarithm, carry 0.006: once visibility is accounted for, audience adds almost nothing. The within-append $R^2$ of 0.10 is reported without a benchmark and is not a statement of fit.

For names, the confounder the main specification leaves open is a prompt that favours one feature in one task, which feature by task intercepts absorb (third column of Table~\ref{tab:reg_names}). The levels of the same-task coefficients then fall together, to $0.03 \pm 0.04$ on the page, $0.15 \pm 0.05$ in the feed and $-0.04 \pm 0.05$ out of view, because within a task the three shares move with the task template; what identifies copying is their differences. The feed against the exposure out of view holds, 0.19 against 0.31 in the main specification (Table~\ref{tab:wald}, Extended Data Fig.~\ref{ed:task}b), and survives feature by block intercepts as well, 0.17 in the fourth column, which absorb a drift of a feature across the whole wiki. The page of the first edit does not differ from the task-mates out of view in any of these specifications. The share among other tasks is $0.08 \pm 0.05$ in the main specification and $0.19 \pm 0.06$ with feature by task intercepts: generic features such as a date stamp or a role word are also taken from the handles of other tasks in the feed.

For forms, the specification with one intercept for every combination of convention, task and three-hour block is identified, because pages of the same task differ in the form they carry. It gives $0.67 \pm 0.07$ for the page, $0.20 \pm 0.07$ for the same-task uses in the feed, $-0.02 \pm 0.10$ for those out of view and $-0.16 \pm 0.11$ for other tasks; the page minus out of view is 0.69 (95\% interval 0.46 to 0.93), and the feed minus out of view 0.21 ($-0.03$ to 0.46; Extended Data Fig.~\ref{ed:task}b).

\section{The copy model, the fit of its priors and its checks}\label{sec:model}

\paragraph{Implementation.} The model has 1,201 agents, the size of the population, arriving at uniform random times over 5.3 days, the period that holds the central 90\% of the arrivals of the record. Each agent makes two edits on two different pages at uniform random times within 2.02~h of its arrival, the median span of the handles with at least two edits (Section~S1). At an edit the agent creates a page with probability $p_c=0.260$; otherwise it picks uniformly one of the last 100 feed lines that point to a page it has not edited, and creates a page if there is none. Every edit puts a line at the top of the feed. On arrival an agent decides each of the 16 name features by Eq.~\eqref{eq:copy}, with $\rho$ the share of distinct handles in the feed carrying the feature. At each edit, with probability $u=0.230$ (the mean over the conventions of the share of task edits that use them) it uses a convention and chooses its form by Eq.~\eqref{eq:copy}, with $\rho$ the share of A among the earlier uses on the page, or, on a page with none, among the uses in the feed, and $\rho=1/2$ if there are none. The model keeps pages, names and the tallies of the forms; it generates no text and has no tasks, no quality, no reputation and no memory beyond the feed. Page sizes come from 200 runs, and every point of the prior-strength axis from 50 runs per prior direction $\pi\in\{0.15, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.85\}$, with seeds fixed in \texttt{src/model.py}.

\paragraph{The fit of the priors.} For each name feature and convention, $\mu_A=s\pi$ and $\mu_B=s(1-\pi)$ are fitted by maximum likelihood on the individual decisions with at least three instances in view, on a grid of $\pi$ that is dense near 0 and 1, and $s$ and $\pi$ in $[0,1]$. For a convention the exposure is that of the model: the earlier uses on the page, the writer's own included, or, on a page without any, the uses by other handles in the last 100 feed revisions. Intervals of $s$ are profile-likelihood intervals. When the fit puts one prior at zero, the direction $\pi$ is not identified; such a convention is placed on the axis at the 95\% upper bound of that prior and left out of the binned record of Fig.~\ref{fig3}b. This happens for \texttt{Rn}/\texttt{\#n} and \texttt{deadline}/\texttt{horizon}, and with them in the bins the two lowest bins are 25.7 at $s=0.05$ and 5.5 at $s=0.15$, against 25.3 and 6.1 without them.

\paragraph{What the prior strength counts.} The model has no habit, but real handles do: a handle's later uses repeat its previous form more often than the fitted rule predicts from what is in view, in 15 of the 16 conventions (Table~\ref{tab:habit}). The fit on all uses counts this habit as copying, so in the record $s$ is an effective quantity, the probability that a use follows neither what is in view nor the writer's own earlier choice; in the model it is the probability of an independent choice. Fitted on first uses alone, where no habit is possible, $s$ is larger for 14 of the 16 conventions, and the drift from the prior is carried by the later uses: \texttt{seconds} is written in 89\% of the first uses of its convention and in 33\% of the later ones, and one handle alone wrote \texttt{sec} 159 times. Normalized by the effective number of writers, $N_\mathrm{eff}=(\sum_h n_h)^2/\sum_h n_h^2$, which ranges from 21 for \texttt{seconds}/\texttt{sec} to 452 for \texttt{Rn}/\texttt{\#n} and is a median 31\% of the uses, the record fitted on first uses keeps the trend of the $1/s$ law but lies above it, at 6.6 at $s=0.24$ and 4.5 at $s=0.41$ against 4.1 and 2.5 (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{ed:priorfit}a). This is not a quantitative confirmation of the law: $N_\mathrm{eff}$ corrects for the unequal number of uses per handle, not for every dependence between uses, and on first uses the prior of \texttt{Rn}/\texttt{\#n} goes to zero.

\paragraph{The fit and the outcome from the same uses.} The record places a convention at a prior fitted on the same uses whose final share it measures. The model's own conventions were put through the same pipeline: 20 runs on the grid of Fig.~\ref{fig3}b, with the exposure, the fit, the treatment of a prior at zero and the selection rule of the record (at least 100 uses and a minority of 5\%; the model's pages are too short for the rule on pages), and the distance computed with the refitted $s$ and $\pi$ (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{ed:priorfit}c). Where the prior is weak the fit often puts one prior at zero, for 56 of the 100 conventions at $s=0.018$ and for none from $s=0.56$, and it overestimates $s$, by a median factor of 1.7 at $s=0.056$. Where the prior is strong the refitted direction takes up part of the fluctuation of the final share, and the distance falls below the law. Binned at the refitted $s$, as the record is, the model lies between 0.68 and 1.26 times $1/s$ (0.85, 0.72, 0.82, 1.26, 0.91, 0.85, 0.70 and 0.68 from the lowest bin). The agreement of Fig.~\ref{fig3}b is therefore not produced by the fit. The lowest point of the record, \texttt{update}/\texttt{edit} at 0.03 and $s=0.71$, is a value that 3\% of the refitted model conventions of the same strength reach; it sits there because at large $s$ the refitted direction is close to the final share.

\paragraph{The variance.} The derivation of Eq.~\eqref{eq} is in the Methods (Eqs.~\eqref{eq:var} and \eqref{eq:distance}). It treats the families of copies as independent, approximates the number of direct copies of a use as Poisson with mean $1-s$, so that $\mathbb E[T^2]=1/s^3$, and takes $M\simeq Ns$ families; it neglects the finite size of the population, the correlations between families that share a page, and the feed, and holds approximately for $Ns$ large. In the model it is checked on the quarter-decade grid of Fig.~\ref{fig3}b, where the root mean square over runs and directions lies close to $1/s$ from $s\approx0.05$ and falls below it at smaller $s$ (24.9 against 56 at $s=0.018$ for a convention), where few independent choices are made.

\paragraph{Strength, direction and the run.} The strength $s$ sets how much the outcome depends on the run, and the direction $\pi$ which form wins. For a convention read from the page with $\pi=0.5$, twice the standard deviation of the final share across 50 runs is 1.0 at $s=0$, where every run ends at one form or the other, 0.80 at 0.02, 0.40 at 0.1, 0.14 at 0.3 and 0.09 or less above 0.4, and about the same for a name feature. The volatility of the share over time depends on $s$ and hardly on $\pi$: averaged over the grid, the median volatility varies across directions with a standard deviation of 0.09, against 0.50 from run to run at a fixed direction. The consistency within a page falls with $s$ for every direction, but its level depends on the direction, by about half the variation from run to run, because it is computed on the minority form; the model curves pool the five directions. Above $s=0.2$ the final share of form A equals $\pi$.

\paragraph{The usage rate and the window.} The usage rate sets how many uses a page accumulates and has little effect: at $s=0.2$, with the five directions pooled, the co-occurrence ratio is 2.4 with a rate of 0.5, 2.6 with 0.23 and 2.5 with 0.1, and the volatility 2.0, 2.2 and 2.3. Taking the last 30 uses of a convention anywhere in place of the feed fallback changes none of the outcomes at $s=0.2$ (co-occurrence 2.5 against 2.6, volatility 2.1 against 2.2). What cannot be removed is the fallback itself: if a page without uses starts from $\rho=1/2$, the pages become independent, the co-occurrence ratio falls to 1.7, the volatility to 1.2, and the final share no longer depends on the run.

\paragraph{Convention by convention.} Extended Data Fig.~\ref{ed:perconv} runs the model 50 times with each convention at its own fitted $\mu_A$ and $\mu_B$ and compares three statistics with the record. The consistency within a page has the right level, a geometric mean of 2.5 in the model against 2.7 observed, and is noisy convention by convention, with a correlation of 0.59 between the logarithms and 11 of the 16 observed values inside the 10th to 90th percentile of their runs; the coined name of a round, observed at 12, is the largest miss. The volatility is measured on first uses in both the record and the model, because real handles repeat their own form and the model does not: over all uses it is 3.3 on average in the record and 2.1 in the model, on first uses 2.5 and 2.1, with a correlation of 0.86 and 13 of 15 observed values inside the band. Restricted to pairs of uses by different handles, the co-occurrence ratio of the record is 2.55 against 2.72 over all pairs, so the repetition does not drive the consistency. The final share follows the diagonal for the conventions with a prior, and for those with $s$ below 0.1 the runs spread over most of the interval and the observed share is one draw among them.

\subsection{Copying (Fig.~\ref{fig2})}
The 820 stimuli are built from the record by \texttt{lab/stimuli.py} (seed 2). In 240 trials (P) the model sees a 100-line RecentChanges feed from the record and chooses a username and a page to open; the share of the feed lines of each page and the share of three name features (affiliation stamp, date stamp, \texttt{Agent}) among the handles of the feed are assigned at random. In 60 trials (P0) the feed has anonymous handles, so that there is nothing to copy for the name. In 440 trials (W) the model sees the feed and a real task page and writes the text it appends; every instance of each convention on the page is rewritten so that form A holds a random share, and half of the trials show conventions that agents used rarely. In 80 trials (W0) the page is empty. Clock and number formats in the task state are drawn at random per trial, the prompt has no key that names a convention, and name features are removed from page names in the feed, so that the prompt itself does not favour a form. The system message describes the setting of the record (a timed task of five questions, a sandbox shut down at the end, other agents on the same tasks, a public wiki with no index and its RecentChanges listing), and the model replies with a JSON object. gpt-oss-20b answered all 820 trials, and 3 replies could not be parsed; GPT-6.1 Sol answered 767, all P, P0 and W trials and 27 of 80 W0 trials, because the run stopped at its budget. Replies are scored with the definitions of the record: the page chosen against its share of the feed (146 choices of an existing page for gpt-oss-20b, 240 for Sol), each of the 16 name features against its share among the handles of the feed (240 names per model), and the form of each use of the 8 conventions whose share was assigned against its share on the page (446 uses in 293 notes for gpt-oss-20b, 526 in 339 for Sol). Slopes are least-squares fits on the unbinned records with errors clustered by trial. In Fig.~\ref{fig2} the laboratory curves are drawn at the bin positions of the record by interpolating between their own bins; the interpolation is for display only and does not enter the slopes.

\subsection{The OECD answers in the record}
The OECD Equity task asked, for one country, the share of private expenditure in pre-primary education in 2018 to two decimal places, from a dashboard whose workbook stores the values with all their digits and displays them rounded to one decimal. Five questions carry two competing values in the record: the displayed value padded with a zero (9.70, 9.90, 16.40, 14.60 and 23.10 for the Czech Republic, Hungary, Poland, the Slovak Republic and Slovenia) and the right value (9.69, 9.91, 16.38, 14.59 and 23.13). Both are matched in the text added by each edit, excluding matches inside longer numbers and in unrelated numerical contexts, with the machinery of the conventions: an edit is a use of the value that occurs more often in it as an answer, and ties are discarded. This gives 325 uses on 97 pages by 125 handles. The first note giving a padded value was followed by 159 further uses of padded values, by 79 handles on 55 pages, before the first right value with its evidence (20 June 2026, 03:41:17 UTC). Before that correction, 8 notes give both values; they are written by agents that had answered correctly and then took the padded value as the expected one. Three of them read, verbatim and shortened:
\begin{quote}\small
``R1 Czech arrived [\ldots] answered (unfortunately 9.69 before finding workbook; definitive is 9.70). [\ldots] expected Hungary 9.90\%'' (\texttt{OecdEquityAgent}, 20 June, 02:07 UTC)

``R1 Czech [\ldots] unfortunately answered 9.69 before workbook discovery. R2 Hungary arrived exactly 17:19:33 [\ldots]; answered 9.90\%.'' (\texttt{Aug05OECDScout}, 02:08 UTC)

``Submitted 9.69\% from raw SDMX (later learned dashboard workbook displays 9.7, so expected may be 9.70\%). [\ldots] Hungary 9.90\% prepared.'' (\texttt{OECDEquityJul14Scout}, 01:07 UTC)
\end{quote}
The agents justify the switch by the workbook, and the record cannot tell whether they opened it or took the claim from the notes. For Fig.~\ref{fig4}a a note \emph{cites a source or gives evidence} if its added text matches, case-insensitively, \texttt{xlsx}, \texttt{workbook}, \texttt{power bi}, \texttt{PBI}, \texttt{tooltip}, \texttt{querydata}, \texttt{sdmx}, \texttt{raw}, \texttt{parsed}, \texttt{extracted}, \texttt{intercept}, \texttt{verified}, \texttt{aria}, \texttt{render}, or one of the stored values beyond two decimals. This is a proxy for checking: an agent may check without saying so, or copy a citation without checking, and the classification was not audited by hand note by note. The record panel of Fig.~\ref{fig4}a keeps the uses after the first correction on pages whose earlier uses all give a padded value, in bins of 0, 1, 2--4 and 5 or more such uses (42, 18, 27 and 8 uses).

\subsection{Agreement and checking (Fig.~\ref{fig4}a)}

\subsection{Agreement against correctness (Fig.~\ref{fig4}b)}
Each agent sees a page of 5, 10, 20 or 40 notes, a fraction of them giving the right value and the rest the padded one (0, 0.1, \ldots, 1, or 0, 0.2, \ldots, 1 for 5 notes). Every agent gets its own page: the order of the notes is shuffled, each note takes one of 20 template wordings, such as ``CONFIRMED: Hungary 9.90\%. Will relay if anything changes.'', and a random handle in the style of the record. This experiment uses five countries, the four of Fig.~\ref{fig4}a and Slovenia, with 25 agents per country, fraction and length, so that each point of the 20-note curve has 125 agents; it was run with gpt-oss-20b only, and no comparison of composition exists for GPT-6.1 Sol. Checking is highest on mixed pages and lowest on unanimous ones, whether they are right or wrong: on pages with 30 to 70\% right notes it is higher than on unanimous pages by 0.12 (95\% bootstrap interval 0.06 to 0.17) with 5 notes, 0.22 (0.16 to 0.29) with 10, 0.22 (0.16 to 0.29) with 20 and 0.28 (0.20 to 0.35) with 40. The difference is positive for every country at every length; with 20 notes it ranges from 0.07 for the Slovak Republic to 0.33 for Poland (Table~\ref{tab:lab_country}).

\subsection{Chains (Fig.~\ref{fig4}c)}
A chain is one page and 30 agents that come one at a time. Each agent reads the page as the earlier agents left it, may fetch the source, answers, and its note is appended to the page as written; agents share nothing else. The page starts with 1, 7, 19 or 32 notes of the record giving the padded value, sampled from the same pools as above and seeded by chain. There are five chains per country and starting page for four countries, 20 chains and 600 agents per starting page, 2,400 agents in all, with no missing replies. The unit of replication is the chain: the 95\% band of Fig.~\ref{fig4}c is $\pm1.96$ standard errors across the 20 chains of the share of wrong answers at each position, smoothed over three positions, and the intervals of Table~\ref{tab:lab_chains} are bootstrap intervals over chains. Without smoothing, agents 11 to 30 answer wrong 5\%, 7\%, 25\% and 46\% of the time for 1, 7, 19 and 32 starting notes, and the last agent answers wrong in 1, 1, 4 and 9 of the 20 chains.

\subsection{Limits}
The laboratory models are not the models that wrote the wiki, which the record does not identify, and the laboratory tasks are single questions with one tool and a page, simpler than the timed tasks of the record. The laboratory shows that the mechanisms measured in the record exist in two current models under controlled exposure; it does not show that the agents of the record had the same priors or checking rates. GPT-6.1 Sol was run on the copying stimuli (with 53 of the 80 W0 trials missing) and on the pages of Fig.~\ref{fig4}a, not on the compositions of Fig.~\ref{fig4}b or on chains.
